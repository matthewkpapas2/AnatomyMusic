## 4. Findings, most important first
1. **Advanced is thin on house for structural reasons, not mainly the voice rule.** Of 111 Advanced techniques skipped
   across the 20 directions, **65 (59%) can't apply to the pair at all**: Key-lock-off blend (20) and Tempo ride (16)
   need a tempo gap, Harmonic key change (17) needs distant keys with a pivot chord, Trade phrases (6) needs bar-by-bar
   chord agreement. **39 (35%)** fell under the sound floor. **Only 7 (6%)** were blocked by the one-voice minimum.
   v7 guessed the voice floor was the cause (tech debt #27); on house, it mostly isn't. Same-tempo, same-key house
   pairs, which are the common case, effectively get four Advanced candidates: Fake-out, Build into build, Silence
   switch and Wormhole.
2. **Fake-out drop leaves a hole.** Its "echo fills the missing drop bar" decays to more than 20 dB under the mix:
   1.0 s of dead air on the demo, 2.3 s on Drugs From Amsterdam → Losing It and → Turn Off The Lights (scores 48).
   The paper check agrees, failing Fake-out on Flow ("sags about 1.3 s") on other pairs, so this is a real generator
   problem (echo feedback 0.5 isn't enough to carry a bar), not a harness artifact.
3. **Compatibility doesn't discriminate.** Every one of the 20 directions scores 82–98 ("Good" or "Great"), including
   Rumble ↔ Animals, which the test list picked as un-beatmatchable (9% apart; the tempo part scores 40, "transitions
   that reset the beat only"). The 35% weight on "best transition found"
   lifts every pair that has one clean cut or echo idea. This is weight-fitting work for the dataset phase.
4. **Some drops are wrong, and transitions inherit it.** Rumble's 0:19 "drop" sits in a stop-start intro (Rumble
   itself falls 25–40 dB at 16.0, 17.5–18.2 and 19.5 s), so every Animals → Rumble Beginner idea lands in Rumble's own
   gaps (Filter riser 0, Tape stop and Echo 58). Opus (radio edit) gets only low-confidence hits, so Strobe → Opus
   shared-chord drops land with *less* bass (−5.8 dB). 20 of 286 ideas land on a gap-only entry (confidence 0.30).
5. **A Beginner blend can stack two singers.** Kelly → Bieber Blend into the drop has 3.9 s of two singers (score 61);
   Beginner has no one-voice minimum by design, and the measurement says that costs. Marea → Latch Blend is 35 ms off
   the beat (score 62), the only clearly audible beatmatch error measured; Double drop there has 2.7 s of two singers
   despite passing the paper voice floor (0.45).
6. **The vocal detector hears synths as singers** (tech debt #15, now with numbers): Opus 94% sung, Levels 82%,
   Nanana 86%. It inflates the harness's mid-line count (56) and pushes Intermediate and Advanced ideas on
   instrumental house under the one-voice floors.
7. **Where Have You Been → Feel So Close gets nothing above Beginner.** Of the candidates that could apply, most
   miss their floor by 1–8 points (Drop swap and Loop roll 72 vs 73, Silence switch 72 vs 75), a few by 18–19
   (Shared-chord drop 55, Wormhole 56), and Fake-out and Trade phrases pass the sound floor (77, 79) but fail the
   one-voice minimum. Both are 86–89%-sung EDM-pop songs.
8. **Silence switch can land quieter.** Where You Are → Nanana lands 6.7 LU quieter (score 20) and still passed the
   paper Flow row: `planLevels()` didn't predict it (tech debt #25).

## 5. Caveats
- Three test songs are still the wrong versions (Losing It, Drugs From Amsterdam and Opus are radio or video edits),
  so the transition results for pairs using them describe those edits, not the DJ versions.
- Strobe is analyzed only to 10:00 (the 600 s cap).
- The harness score rule is new and uncalibrated. Treat a 1–2 point difference as noise.
- Node and the browser decode differently (ffmpeg vs `decodeAudioData`), so the profiles here and the page can differ
  slightly. The reference-pair compatibility matched exactly, which says the difference is small.
